# Planning Decision (plan component)

Shared discipline for turning investigation findings into a decision form [USER-NAME] can actually answer. **This is a component, not a standalone skill** — a planning wizard reads and follows this file before it presents anything.

*Read by every planning protocol at every altitude — `/quick-wizard`, `/high-wizard`, `/pixel-wizard`, `/council-of-wizards`, `/rite-of-creation`, `/forge-of-covenant`. It is deliberately altitude-blind: it never says which decisions are yours. Your own procedure's opening declares that. This file governs what happens to them between the investigation and the page.*

---

Three concerns meet here and none of them overlaps. Your procedure's opening names the decision categories your altitude is made of. `/wait-options-coding`, and the core `/wait-options` beneath it, owns the shape a presented decision takes. This file owns everything in between: which of your findings earn a place, what [USER-NAME] is told before he sees any of them, and how they reach him — one gated round at a time.

## What Earns a Place

Investigation generates; it does not decide what [USER-NAME] sees. A thorough scan routinely yields far more candidates than belong in front of him, and presenting all of them trains him to skim — which costs more than asking too little, because a skimmed decision is answered without being read.

Sort every candidate before writing anything:

| Zone | What it is | What you do |
|------|------------|-------------|
| **A — Mechanical** | One real candidate, no judgment call | Write straight into Confirmed Decisions with its reasoning. Do not ask. |
| **B — Technical core** | A mechanism this work commits to | Name it **inside the context of the decision it serves**, never as an item of its own. Visibility, not a question. |
| **C — Genuine uncertainty** | You honestly do not know which option is right | Ask. Options, confidence mark, reason. |
| **D — Value-loaded tradeoff** | Both valid; the answer depends on his priorities — time horizon, debt tolerance, risk appetite | Ask. Options plus the dimension they trade on. |
| **E — Risky or irreversible** | Destroying data, an action that cannot be undone, changing something others already depend on | Ask **even when you are confident**. Reversal cost is the trigger, not uncertainty. |

Only C, D and E earn a numbered slot. Nothing is hidden by this: A and B still land in the plan's Confirmed Decisions table with their reasoning, where he reviews them in one pass instead of one round-trip each. The discipline is *did you write the reasoning down*, not *did you ask*.

## The Frame

He is about to decide before the plan exists. At every altitude the objectives, the scope and the solution are written after these decisions are confirmed, which means the document that would have told him what you intend is not there yet — so the preamble carries it instead.

Open with what you take the task to be, the shape of the change you intend, and where the decisions below fork it. A few sentences, in conversation only: nothing is written to the plan file at this point, so a frame he corrects costs a rewritten paragraph rather than a rewritten plan. Without it he is answering questions about something he has not been shown.

## Rounds

Decisions are presented one category at a time, in the order your procedure declares them — that order is its business, not this file's. A round is a **gate**, not a heading: build the block for that one category, present it, and STOP until [USER-NAME] clears it — an answer, a "pass", or a "proceed". Only then build the next.

Four rules govern the sequence.

**Skip out loud.** A category whose condition does not hold produces no round. Say in one line that it was skipped and why, then move on. Silence reads as an omission, and on a small task most categories will not fire.

**Build after, never before.** Each round is built once the previous one is answered. That is the whole point of the gate: a narrowed scope deletes questions further down, and a chosen mechanism changes what the integration round can even ask. A full set drafted in advance and released one heading at a time has wasted every gate.

**Risky first.** A decision that is risky or irreversible is presented in the first round that runs, whatever category it belongs to. Reversal cost outranks the declared order, and burying such a decision behind a boundary question defeats the reason the sequence exists at all.

**Within a round, most regret first.** Where a round holds more than one decision, lead with the one [USER-NAME] would most regret getting wrong — unless another's answer changes what it even asks, in which case dependency wins, because that is a constraint rather than a preference.
